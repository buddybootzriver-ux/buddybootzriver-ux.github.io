The Adventures of Buddy Bootz & Sir Drinks-A’Lot

A chaotic fantasy adventure about a girl, a man, and a river that refuses to behave.

The river doesn’t follow rules. Buddy doesn’t either. Sir is stuck managing both.

Read the First Chapter

Buddy Bootz lives in a treehouse on the edge of the Rambunctious River—a river that reacts instead of flows. When a simple repair goes wrong, she and Sir Drinks-A’Lot are pulled into something that builds, shifts, and refuses to stay contained. What begins as a broken morning turns into a journey neither of them understands—and the further they go, the less the world behaves the way it should.

Buddy Bootz

She acts first, thinks later, and fixes problems by making new ones.

Sir Drinks-A’Lot

He keeps things running, keeps Buddy alive, and fails at stopping either.

Together

She creates the problem. He gets them out of it. The river makes sure neither of them is in control.

The World

The Rambunctious River doesn’t follow direction. It reacts—to movement, to decisions, to them. Sometimes it helps. Sometimes it traps. Once you’re in it, you don’t control where it takes you.

Read a Moment from the River

The pump coughed.

A blast of rusty water shot straight into Buddy’s face.

She went still.

Water dripped off her nose.

“…That was personal.”

Below her, something crashed through the brush.

Sir Drinks-A’Lot shoved into view, already irritated.

“I was fine,” he said, brushing leaves off his sleeve, “until the forest got involved.”

“Good,” Buddy said. “Hand me the big wrench.”

He picked the correct one immediately.

“This one?”

“Yes. That one.”

Buddy set it against the pipe and leaned.

Nothing.

She leaned harder.

Still nothing.

“You ever get the feeling machines know when you’re close to winning?”

“Yes,” Sir said. “They resent it.”

She held out her hand.

Sir stared at it.

“Absolutely not.”

Buddy didn’t move.

He sighed—and handed over the flask.

She took a drink.

Then poured a splash straight into the pump.

“Buddy.”

“Negotiating.”

“With plumbing.”

“With attitude.”

The pump rattled violently—

then released a deep mechanical BURP.

Clear water flowed smoothly.

Buddy threw her arms wide.

“Ha!”

Sir stared at the pipe.

“That is not how plumbing works.”

“It worked.”

Coffee Time

Somewhere between strong coffee and a bad decision— the talking started. Not planned. Not structured. Not particularly helpful. Still… consistent. Buddy says what she means. Sir says what he can manage. The river listens anyway. This is Coffee Time.
