"This is where it starts going wrong."

Nothing about this was supposed to involve the river.

Buddy Bootz solved most problems the way other people stepped over puddles: by putting one boot squarely in the middle of them.

The town pump had stopped pumping, which was the sort of crisis that made a person look important without necessarily making them useful. Buddy planted her hands on her hips and studied the crank wheel.

"It is, technically, still a pump," Sir Drinks-A’Lot said, peering at it with the grave expression of a man who had already seen a worse day before breakfast.

"Then why is the handle smoking?"

"Because," he said, "the handle appears to be fighting the water."

Buddy nodded as if this were a normal sentence to hear in a civic emergency. She grabbed the crank, gave it a heroic shove, and immediately discovered two things: the wheel was jammed, and the river was now audible from the square.

Somewhere downstream, water roared back as if offended by being invited into public service.

"You were supposed to fix this," Buddy said.

Sir Drinks-A’Lot straightened his vest, reached into his coat, and produced a wrench, a spoon, and a small prayer.

"I am," he said. "I am fixing it into a different problem."
