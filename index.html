Buddy Bootz stood on the top step of a tall ladder, one hand steadying the metal housing while the other worked a screwdriver into a stubborn seam.

Her glitter-brown boots were planted carefully. Her red dress had already picked up grease at the hem. Her gold crown sat slightly crooked in her short auburn hair, catching sunlight through the branches.

"Alright," Buddy said. "You've made your point."

The pump coughed. A blast of rusty water shot straight into her face.

Buddy went still. Water dripped off her nose. "...That felt personal."

I'm Buddy Bootz.

I write stories, poems, and the occasional wonderfully bad idea that seems perfectly reasonable right up until the moment it isn't.

My stories don't sit in a neat line. They wander. They argue with themselves. They start off acting like it's all jokes and light chaos and then, somewhere in the middle, they get real quiet like they've just remembered something they weren't supposed to say out loud.

That's kind of my favorite part.

I lean into humor hard. Not the polished kind. The kind that slips in sideways when things are getting too heavy to hold straight. The kind that laughs a little too loudly in the wrong moment because silence would mean something else entirely. My writing trusts that tension. It lets it breathe.

As a writer, I'm drawn to contrasts. Humor beside heartbreak. Wonder beside reality. Chaos beside kindness. I think that's where some of the most interesting stories live.

And then there are my poems.

My poems don't joke around. Well... not always. Some are playful, curious, and a little absurd. They find wonder in ordinary things and aren't afraid to smile at the strange corners of life.

Others get raw. Darn emotional. The kind of emotional that doesn't ask permission or wait for the room to be ready. They come straight from lived experience, from memory that still has heat in it, from feelings that didn't finish processing before they turned into language.

They don't explain themselves. They don't soften the edges. They land how they land. Sometimes they feel like something breaking. Sometimes like something finally telling the truth without flinching. Either way, they're honest.

If my writing stays with you a little longer than you expected, then I've done exactly what I set out to do.
