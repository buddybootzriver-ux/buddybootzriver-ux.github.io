Transmission #03

The Quiet Parts

Sometimes the forest holds its breath. It’s not waiting for something to happen. It’s waiting for us to notice it already has.

Sir and I were out by the lower bend today. The river was doing that thing where it pretends to be a mirror, reflecting everything so perfectly you forget which way is up. It was so still it felt like a dare.

"Do you ever get the feeling being around you has damaged my ability to trust a quiet morning?" Sir asked.

He's observant. That's the trouble. He sees straight through the things I haven't even decided to show yet. But he stayed. We both did. Just watching the water pretend to be something else for a while.

Transmission #02

Gerald’s Heist

A squirrel stole my screwdriver today. He didn't just pick it up—he looked me dead in the eye, tucked it under his arm like a professional, and launched himself into the oak.

Sir says he escalated the situation. I think he was just under pressure. We're all trying to fix something, aren't we? Even the squirrels.

I named him Gerald. Sir shut his eyes for a long second and told me not to. But the name is already working. Gerald is a thief, but he's a thief with a name now, and that makes him part of the story.

Transmission #01

The Pump’s Opinion

The water pump bolted to the treehouse was doing its best to come apart in public today. It sounded less like machinery and more like a personal objection.

I tried logic. I tried a screwdriver. Finally, I tried negotiating with attitude. I poured a small splash of whiskey directly into the intake.

Sir stared at me like I'd just renamed the moon. "That is not how plumbing works," he said.

It released a deep mechanical BURP and started running clear water immediately. It worked. That’s the only part that ever really matters, isn't it?
